Understanding Write-Ahead Logging

By Jane Researcher

Write-ahead logging (WAL) is a family of techniques for providing atomicity and durability in database systems. The central idea is that changes to data files must be written only after those changes have been logged, that is, after the log records describing the changes have been flushed to permanent storage.

Because the log is written sequentially, it is far cheaper than flushing every changed page to disk on each transaction commit. On recovery, the system replays the log to bring the database back to a consistent state, redoing committed transactions and undoing those that never committed.

This approach underlies crash recovery in PostgreSQL, SQLite, and many other engines, and it is the reason a durable commit does not require a full data-file sync.
